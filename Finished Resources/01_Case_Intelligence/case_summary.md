# Case Intelligence Report: PortFlow Container Terminal: Yard and Gate Flow
**Discipline**: Supply Chain and Logistics Engineering | **Difficulty**: Championship

### Problem Statement
PortFlow is a Canadian container terminal linking shipping lines, trucking, rail and inland customers. Today the yard holds 21,120 TEU against 24,000 TEU effective capacity (88%), containers dwell 6 days, and trucks take 72 minutes per visit (40 queuing, 32 inside). Volume will grow 20% in five years. The terminal spends $6M a year on overtime and $8M on delay and service penalties. Release and schedule information is not shared consistently, planning runs across separate systems on fixed rules, and the yard system knows a container's stack but not its exact position. One 15-acre parcel (about 4,000 TEU) could be added, phased around live operations.

### Core Decision Question
Which package, within the $10M upfront budget, attacks PortFlow's core issue so that at +20% volume the yard runs at or below 85% utilization and trucks turn in under 45 minutes by year five?

### Objective
Year 5 at +20% volume: average yard utilization <= 85% and average truck turn time < 45 minutes, within $10M upfront.

### Quantified Gap
The yard is static and blind. Today 21,120 of 24,000 TEU are in use (88%), containers stay 6 days and trucks take 72 minutes. With +20% volume the yard passes its capacity by year 5 unless containers are placed, moved and released faster; the 15-acre parcel alone would still leave about 90%. Absolute change required: 20.59 % of capacity.

### Stated Constraints
- Upfront investment at most $10M CAD; it does not need to be spent in full
- Year-5 objectives: average yard utilization <= 85% and average truck turn time < 45 minutes
- Vessel and truck operations cannot stop while changes are introduced
- Customs holds and missing documentation cannot be overridden by PortFlow
- Up to 8 content slides for a 10-minute presentation
