## Finance
- Upfront $9.0M of the $10M budget, including $0.8M contingency; $1.0M left unspent.
- Added annual costs $1.3M: software subscriptions, support and sensor maintenance ($0.95M), planning-cell analysts ($0.2M), ongoing training ($0.15M).
- Annual savings $6.4M, each with an owner: overtime -40% ($2.4M, Operations Manager) from fewer rehandles, smoother truck peaks and fewer late re-plans; delay and service penalties -50% ($4.0M, Commercial Director) from reliable release information and on-time handovers.
- Five-year net impact = $9.0M + 5 x ($1.3M - $6.4M) = -$16.5M (net savings).
- Main uncertainty: the share of the $8M penalties caused by customs or documentation holds PortFlow cannot override. If penalties fall only 25% ($2.0M), savings are $4.4M and the impact is still -$6.5M. Break-even needs only $3.1M a year of savings.
- Upside not counted: storage revenue from the dwell tariff, and the avoided cost of building on the parcel.

## Operations & Safety
- Storage: dwell 6.0 to 4.7 days keeps year-5 inventory near 19,900 TEU (83% of 24,000) at +20% volume, without new land.
- Container movement: exact slot positions and stacking by planned pickup time cut rehandles, so RTG hours go to productive moves and the 12 RTGs absorb the 20% growth.
- Truck service: appointments spread arrivals (queue 40 to about 14 min); pre-advice and pre-staged containers shorten time inside (32 to about 26 min): about 40 minutes in total, below the 45-minute objective.
- Safety: fewer rehandles and shorter queues reduce truck-crane interaction; hard rules for hazardous, reefer and weight segregation stay locked; every go-live has a safety review.
- Measures, reviewed weekly: utilization, dwell, rehandles per box, queue and inside time, slot adherence, safety incidents and near-misses.

## Supply Chain
- Shipping lines: ETA changes feed one shared plan; export receiving windows and empty-container evacuation agreed.
- Truckers: book pickup slots only when the container is released and ready; live status and a guaranteed window.
- Rail: rail loads pre-staged by train departure; the rail operator sees release status through the same portal.
- Customers: release, hold and ETA alerts to plan replenishment, plus a named contact and escalation process for urgent shipments.
- Customs and documentation holds stay outside PortFlow's control, but they are flagged early so no truck is booked against a held container.

## Organizational Change Management
- Stakeholders: yard operators, gate clerks, planners and supervisors, maintenance, trucking firms, rail, shipping lines, customers and finance.
- Involve staff before tools arrive: operators and planners co-design stacking rules and appointment rules in phase 1.
- Train in paid working time: super-users on every shift, simulator sessions, refresher training budgeted at $0.15M a year.
- Clarify roles: no layoffs from this program; gate clerks and planners move into the planning cell and exception handling; one RACI per process.
- Keep operations running: lane-by-lane and block-by-block rollout, parallel running, go-lives outside vessel peaks and a rollback plan.

## AI & Automation
- Use: AI-assisted stacking and housekeeping recommendations: where to place each incoming container and which overnight moves to make, so boxes due out first sit on top.
- Information needed: exact slot positions (RTG sensors), appointment bookings, release and hold status, vessel and rail schedules, and container attributes (size, weight, reefer, hazardous).
- Oversight: the yard planner reviews and approves every recommendation; hard safety rules cannot be overridden by the model; the Yard Planning Manager owns model performance.
- Performance check: 6 weeks in shadow mode against the current rules, then weekly tracking of rehandles per box, acceptance rate and override reasons; retrain or roll back if rehandles do not fall.
- Set aside: fully automated cranes cost far more than the $10M budget, add safety and labour risk, and do not fix the information gap.
